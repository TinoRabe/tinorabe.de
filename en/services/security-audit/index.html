From the outside, a portal almost always looks fine. Most security problems in Power Pages are not caused by attacks but by configuration: a permission with too wide a scope, a table with too many exposed columns, a sign-in that allows more than intended. Process

When an audit pays off

Your portal uses the Web API, and the column exposure was changed under time pressure.
Microsoft switched off the wildcard * for the exposed columns of the Web API on 14 September 2026. Anyone who requested the one-time 90-day extension is reaching its end now; the exact date is shown in the Power Platform admin center under “Manage exemptions”. After that, requests to affected tables fail. Teams that switched over quickly often exposed more columns than necessary. The audit checks that only the columns your portal really needs are outside.
Microsoft’s security scan shows warnings, and nobody is responsible.
Power Pages comes with its own security scan that checks your site against OWASP-based rules. It finds typical web vulnerabilities such as missing headers or cross-site scripting risks. Whether one customer can see another customer’s cases, however, depends on your data model and your rules. That is what the audit assesses, and it puts the scan results into context as well.
A go-live or a major change is coming up.
Before the first customer access is the cheapest time to review permissions.
You have taken over a portal.
New partner, new team, no documentation: the audit shows the current state before you build on it.
Data protection or internal audit is asking.
The report documents which data can be reached by whom.

The principle

The audit finds, it changes nothing.

The analysis is strictly read-only, with no impact on your operations. The yardstick is risk: a single table permission with too wide a scope on a portal with customer data can mean a reportable data breach.

I carry out audits confidentially. I do not name clients.

Frequently asked questions

How much of our time does the audit take?
About 2.5 hours: kickoff and scoping (60 to 90 minutes) and the results presentation (60 minutes). I do the analysis in between without tying up your team.
Microsoft has a security scan. Isn’t that enough?
Use it, it is a good start. The scan checks your site against general web rules. It cannot know whether your permissions match your business rules. The audit checks exactly that and takes the scan results into account.
Why an external review when we have developers?
Not because your developers are bad. Whoever built a configuration is the worst placed to see its gaps. That applies to me too. That is what the independent outside view is for.
What do you need from us to start?
Read access to the portal configuration and a contact person for questions. We settle the details and the price tier in the kickoff.

When was your portal last reviewed independently?

Tell me in two or three sentences: which portal, which Dynamics 365 apps behind it, is there a specific reason. I reply within one working day.
